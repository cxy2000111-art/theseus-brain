The city goes on with its days. Only that from now on every child knows: there is a star in the night sky, and it is a letter we sent.
The reply may take a few million years. Fortunately, the one thing this city never runs short of is next lives.

### [系-026] 终局结局6 ·「修船」
【原文】
你没有去碰火种，也没有去接舵柄。
你把手伸进外套内袋，掏出一本卷了边的小册子。

《私人小型船舶紧急维修速成手册》。
某一世里，一个盲眼老人握住你的手指，确保你拿稳了它。
那天你把兜里所有的钱都给了他。他说：那就卖给你一本好书吧。
你带着它转了很多世。它从来没有用过。

船长看清封面的时候，提灯的手抖了一下。
「这本……」他说了两个字就停住了。
他退开半步，把灯举高，照亮了船舷下面——
那里的木头是黑的，一直黑到吃水线以下。渡轮在漏。它漏了很久了。

你翻到第三章。第三章讲的是怎么在不停船的情况下换一块板。
「先找承重的那一块，」书上写，「它最先烂，也最难换。
换的时候船会歪。让它歪。歪着也比沉着强。」

你干了整整一夜。
四千一百万粒微光在你头顶浮着，没有一粒替你照亮手底下那块板。
它们只是亮着，等着，像四千一百万个还没轮到的号码。

天亮的时候你把最后一颗钉子敲进去。船不歪了。
你直起腰，发现船长一夜没走——他站在跳板上看你，从头看到尾。

「我守了一辈子，」他说，「守的是别让它沉。」
他把灯放在甲板上，第一次没有把它拎回手里。
「没人告诉过我，它是可以修的。」

你把手册塞回内袋，指腹蹭到封底。封底有一行很浅的铅笔字，
以前从没注意过——不是印上去的，是有人写上去的：

「读到所有事物的所有可能性之后，你就再也做不出选择。
所以别读完。留一页，动手。」

档案没有烧，没有公开，没有被送走，也没有换一个看守。
它只是不漏了。

访问日志的最后一行还在那里。身份栏空着，权限栏写着：正在阅读。
这一次，它下面多了一行新的。
身份栏仍然空着。权限栏写着：**正在修理**。
【新文】
You do not reach for the fire, and you do not take the tiller.
You put your hand into your inside coat pocket and bring out a dog-eared little book.

*A Crash Course in Emergency Repair for Small Private Craft.*
In some life a blind old man closed his fingers over yours to make sure you had a firm hold on it.
That day you gave him everything in your pockets. He said: then let me sell you a good book.
You have carried it through many lives. You have never once used it.

When the captain makes out the cover, the hand holding the lamp shakes.
“This—” He gets two words out and stops.
He steps back half a pace and lifts the lamp higher, lighting the hull below the rail—
the wood there is black, black all the way down past the waterline. The ferry is leaking. It has been leaking a long time.

You turn to chapter three. Chapter three is about replacing a plank without stopping the boat.
“Find the load-bearing one first,” the book says. “It rots first, and it is the hardest to change.
The boat will list while you do it. Let it list. Listing still beats sinking.”

You work the whole night.
Forty-one million points of light hang above you and not one of them lights the plank under your hands.
They only shine, and wait, like forty-one million numbers whose turn has not come.

At daybreak you drive the last nail in. The boat stops listing.
You straighten up and find the captain has not left all night—he is standing on the gangway watching you, and has watched from start to finish.

“I kept this my whole life,” he says. “What I kept was: don’t let it sink.”
He sets the lamp down on the deck, and for the first time does not pick it back up.
“Nobody ever told me it could be repaired.”

You push the manual back into your pocket, and your fingertip catches the back cover. There is a faint line of pencil there
you had never noticed—not printed. Written by somebody:

“Once you have read every possibility of everything, you can never make a choice again.
So don’t finish reading. Leave a page, and get to work.”

The archive was not burned, not published, not sent away, and not handed to a different keeper.
It simply stopped leaking.

The last line of the access log is still there. The identity field blank, the permission field reading: reading.
This time there is a new line under it.
The identity field is still blank. The permission field reads: **under repair**.
